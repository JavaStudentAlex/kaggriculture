# Aspen Vale: submission and predictor review

Reviewed 2026-09-29 Europe/Berlin. Remote evidence snapshot: **2026-09-28
23:12:28 UTC**, saved in `REVIEW_20260929_EVIDENCE.json`. This is an audit and
integration specification; no policy, running experiment, or submission was changed.

## Recommendation

Keep Aspen Vale's `tetsutani_demand_0927` backbone, `_SR_MARGIN=12`, and rival
emulator. The next candidate should test the predictor's always-enabled guard,
first independently and then with `_CA_MARGIN=-22`. Compare the reference model
and calibrated September 26 model using genuinely different bundles. Do not select
the newer model using the currently configured round 3: it resolves to the old
model's bundle. Production improvements, especially tomatoes, need their own tests.

### P1: Aspen Vale never invokes its bundled predictor

The submitted `policy_graph.json` disables `farmer`, `hands`, `market`, and
`oracle_guard`. `graph_runtime.py:273-279` therefore sets `oracle_needed=False`.
Its saved validation confirms **zero forecasts**, with `oracle_live=False`.
The archive and its entrypoint match the manifest, and all 37 runtime pins match.
This is the submitted configuration, not an inference failure.

The remote `aspen_guard_graph.json` does enable the guard without family-specific
suppression. Its Maple Crest build has eight completed loader-validation games,
zero reported oracle errors, 463 forecasts in the final game's tracker, and one
starter-opponent fidelity comparison with identical cash. This proves that the
integration executes; it does not establish competitive improvement.

The **local evolution queue still differs** from that candidate:
`research/procedural_graph/evolution_queue_ladder.json:16-18` contains
`_OG_ITEMS: []` for both recognized engine families. Remove those overrides from
future oracle-on candidates/queue edits. Keeping the predictor running in every
game does not require selling on every forecast. Exact emulation can inform
current-turn timing while the neural forecast informs later turns.

### P1: the claimed six flips are estimates, not six replayed wins

`oracle_steps.py` produces forecasts from recorded observations and records the
original actions (`:88-100`). Its `play_guard` then estimates altered revenue;
it does not dispatch `oracle_guard.apply` into a continuing game.

Specific consequences:

- No check for an available order slot (`:123-138`). A synthetic reproduction
  with ten occupied slots gave **one credited four-unit sale** in the analysis
  and **zero added orders** in the real guard.
- Proposed sales are priced from start-of-turn inventory (`:138`), without
  interleaving the two order lists. A correct same-turn prediction does not
  guarantee that our order clears first.
- The estimated rival price loss (`:147-154`) does not replay town consumption,
  intervening fills, changed cash constraints, or either agent's responses.
- Subsequent forecasts stay on the recorded path after the hypothetical sales.
- The 158/168 figure is a roughly 94% event hit rate conditional on proposed
  firings in **31 selected losses**. It is neither whole-ladder precision nor
  proof those firings improve margin. Overlapping four-turn windows can match
  the same rival sale.
- `all_items_0.5` adds egg, carrot, **tomato and melon** (`:43-52`). Its reported
  +$6,097 cannot be attributed specifically to adding eggs and carrots.

The +$5,983 and six flips remain useful hypothesis-generating estimates. Validate
them by actually playing changed actions. Fixed opponent action tapes are a
diagnostic; reactive opponent games are needed for promotion.

### P1: calibration coverage does not match the new agent

The September 26 calibration exists and matches that checkpoint's hash. It was
fit on 600 **feed15-era** games, with zero held-out episodes in the all-games fit.
The report has no positive egg or tomato examples and leaves their factors at
one. On those games the refit's score_4 AUC is worse for strawberry (0.805 to
0.760) and wool (0.693 to 0.681), slightly better for milk (0.633 to 0.642), and
better for carrot (0.939 to 0.962). These are distribution-specific measurements.

This calibration matches the reference model's threshold firing frequencies.
It does **not** turn scores into probabilities or fix their ranking. Use it as
an initial transfer setting, then validate/calibrate on Aspen Vale games with
near-copies, unknown opponents, losses and wins. Split by whole game and, where
possible, opponent identity/date; keep a separate final test set. Choose a model
on paired downstream outcomes, not recency or pooled training AUC alone.

## 2. Agent changes supported by the evidence

Completed round-1 results, retrieved directly from cliproxyapi:

| Comparison set | Aspen Vale W-L-T | Aspen + carrot margin -22 | Mean margin improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 67 recorded opponents, including 36 original wins | 36-31-0 | 38-29-0 | +$64.3/game |
| 31 lost seeds, both seats, substitute live opponents | 51-11-0 | 53-9-0 | +$109.7/game |

All 335 and 310 round-1 game rows respectively were completed and error-free.
The replay change gained four wins but lost two existing wins. Seat breakdown:
replays, 18 to 18 wins in seat 0 and 18 to 20 in seat 1; substitute opponents,
25 to 26 in seat 0 and 26 to 27 in seat 1. The cash sign test is not a test of
general win-rate superiority, and reciprocal seats on one seed are correlated.

The 62-game suite uses six proxy opponents, not all original losing rivals:
Aspen already wins 51 of these games. Treat its positive result as supporting
evidence, with a fresh opponent/seed test still needed. The old-engine Crops and
Tactics candidates lose most of Aspen's original wins; replacing its backbone
with those candidates is poorly supported.

Priority order for policy experiments:

1. **Carrots:** `_CA_MARGIN=-15 -> -22` is the best supported isolated change.
   Test it with the guard as well; their gains are not automatically additive.
2. **Close market races:** use the predictor for stock already in the shed and
   imminent rival sales. Milk/wool/strawberry is the existing three-product
   candidate. Evaluate eggs and carrots as a separate expansion.
3. **Tomato investment:** test the existing `_CXTB_MIN_REVENUE` gate at 7,000
   against 9,000, with 6,000 exploratory. The evidence estimates roughly $7,500
   investment cost; a lower gross-revenue gate is not inherently profitable.
   The 7,785 projected/11,004 realized rival tomato case makes a targeted test
   reasonable, not a universal lower threshold.
4. **Tomato capacity:** the gate only controls a zero-to-ten-plant branch.
   `_cxtb_qualifies` rejects farms already growing tomatoes and routes already
   scheduling them (`engines/tetsutani_demand_0927/agent/main.py:6763-6786`).
   Lowering it cannot fix Ichika's 18-versus-10 plant advantage. That needs a
   route/production change including seed cash, watering, harvest labour and
   storage. `_CXTB_OUR_UNITS` is a revenue-projection parameter, not a command
   to plant more tomatoes.
5. **Emulator tolerance:** first divergence currently drops a hypothesis for the
   whole game. That limits usefulness against tuned copies. A future approximate
   tracker could retain uncertainty after small market deviations, but public
   inventory flow alone does not establish every hidden state or future policy.
   Do not silently continue treating a deviated engine as an exact opponent.

The 96-turn predictor covers four days. Tomato first yield is eight days after
planting in the pinned engine, so it cannot by itself price the day-18 planting
decision's day-26 harvest. Use visible production and the engine's longer-horizon
economics for that decision; use the predictor nearer to harvest for sale timing.

## 3. Concrete predictor integration

Apply this **graph edit** to a copy of the exact Aspen Vale graph using
`graph_edits.apply_edit(graph, edit, graph_edits.catalog())`. This is an edit
object, not a replacement full `policy_graph.json`:

```json
{
  "channels": {"oracle_guard": true},
  "parameters": {
    "_OG_ITEMS": ["MILK", "WOOL", "STRAWBERRY"],
    "_OG_SCORE": 0.5,
    "_OG_STRONG_SCORE": 0.6,
    "_OG_BATCH": 4,
    "_OG_KEEP": 2,
    "_OG_PRICE_RATIO": 0.75,
    "_OG_MAX_ORDERS": 2,
    "_OG_FROM_STEP": 256,
    "_OG_TO_STEP": 696
  }
}
```

Leave the existing production channels disabled so they pass through Aspen's
backbone. No additional rival-counter stage is required for this baseline.
For the combined candidate add `"engine_parameters": {"_CA_MARGIN": -22.0}`.

The existing runtime already provides the necessary sequence:

1. Observe the real game from turn 0, including our private state only, and build
   the 256 completed rows. Forecast every turn once sufficient context exists.
2. Produce the backbone action and expose the forecast in `farm_state`.
3. Let the guard propose additional sales, accounting for planned sells,
   reserve stock, price floor and the ten-slot limit.
4. Sanitize; let the emulator optimize order positions when its hypothesis is
   still valid; record the **final dispatched action** for the next observation.

`score_4` is the maximum predicted log1p sale amount over four horizons, or its
calibrated score, **not P(sale)**. The raw model quantities also should not be
treated as exact volumes. `_OG_BATCH=4` is **not a hard limit**: at score >=0.6
the guard sells all free stock. A verified example with ten milk and keep-two
sells eight. Make that branch explicit when tuning or proposing a capped variant.

This guard adds sales; it does not comprehensively optimize existing orders.
If the engine already sells all available stock, the guard adds nothing. When
emulation is unavailable, extending sale timing should compare the baseline,
an earlier existing sale, a small extra sale, and waiting. Evaluate candidate
lists using actual slot mechanics and plausible opponent-sale scenarios, with
town consumption, resource needs and uncertainty included. Prefer an estimated
improvement in our cash margin over merely responding to a high forecast score.

For packaging, `make_graph_submission.py` already accepts explicit `--graph`,
`--checkpoint`, `--calibration`, `--name`, `--validate` and `--fidelity` arguments.
Use `models/ttm_c256_h96_ft_2026-09-26` with
`research/procedural_graph/calibration/ttm_c256_h96_ft_2026-09-26/own_games/calibration.json`
for the refit. The reference checkpoint is
`research/procedural_graph/hazel_runtime/checkpoint`, without a calibration file.
The builder copies config, labels, weights and scaler together, includes the
NumPy implementation, and refreshes source pins. Check the extracted artifact's
model/calibration hashes; the shared gauntlet cache is the separate failure above.

## 4. Validation before promotion

Use a small comparison matrix: unchanged Aspen; carrots alone; reference guard
alone; reference guard plus carrots; calibrated refit with the same guard; refit
guard plus carrots. Test extra products and tomato changes separately after
establishing this baseline. Tune only on development games.

- First reproduce recorded Aspen games and baseline cash. Check losses **and**
  wins for regressions using the actual guard and executed fills.
- Then use frozen reactive opponents, both seats and fresh held-out seeds,
  including exact engines and tuned copies. Report completed/missing/error
  counts, W-L-T, gained/lost wins, margin changes and seat/opponent breakdowns.
- Keep hash identities in job/result records. Prebuild bundles, isolate result
  files and reject missing/error jobs; do not trust summary counts that silently
  discard errors. Never use stale round-1 reports as round-2 completion evidence.
- Log per-step forecast availability, scores, chosen guard orders, post-emulator
  orders, fills, cash effect and fallback counts. Separate prediction detection
  quality from actual decision value.
- Measure initialization, tail turn latency and overage with predictor **and**
  emulator alive against exact engines. The saved eight-game starter/random
  test is insufficient for that worst case; those opponents may drop emulator
  hypotheses early. Its 101-142 ms game-wide means do not bound worst-case turns.
- Validate the extracted archive with no source-repository dependency, matching
  scaler/labels/model/calibration, live forecasts and zero oracle errors.

At the evidence snapshot, only the round-1 summaries were present. Inspected
round-2/3 caches had no oracle-labelled results yet, and `fresh_av` had no rows.
There was no round-4 script/report in the inspected evidence/validation locations.
No competitive oracle winner can be selected from that snapshot.
